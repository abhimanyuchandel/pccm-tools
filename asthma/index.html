AIR and SMART: definitions and adult dosing

AIR means anti-inflammatory reliever. SMART means single maintenance and reliever therapy; GINA calls this MART (maintenance-and-reliever therapy). These shortcuts use budesonide-formoterol 160/4.5 mcg per inhalation (Symbicort or Breyna).

ICS dose intensity: common adult maintenance examples

ICS intensity describes the inhaled corticosteroid dose, not symptom severity or the number of drugs in the inhaler. Classify the prescribed scheduled maintenance dose using the exact corticosteroid, strength, device, and daily inhalations. Review as-needed use separately; AIR-only has no scheduled maintenance dose.

The budesonide-formoterol examples describe guideline-supported SMART. Reliever use and the 1-inhalation twice-daily maintenance schedule are off-label in the U.S.; the labeled adult maintenance schedule is 2 inhalations twice daily.

Examples are not dose-equivalent substitutions. For other inhalers, use GINA's product-specific dose categories and U.S. prescribing information. Metered and delivered dose conventions differ; do not compare microgram values across corticosteroids or devices without checking the formulation. A triple inhaler does not automatically mean high-dose ICS. Leave intensity as not assessed if the regimen cannot be verified.

Biologic eligibility: treatment history and clinical review
  • Verify diagnosis, technique, adherence, access, relevant exposures, and comorbid contributors. Poor control on Step 4-5 alone is not sufficient.
  • Review an adequate optimized high-dose ICS-LABA course, usually over 3-6 months, or a documented longitudinal severe-asthma assessment. Asthma that worsened when optimized high-dose ICS-LABA was reduced is another supporting history.
  • Match phenotype, exacerbation history, and comorbidities to the chosen agent. Verify product eligibility, contraindications, dosing, and coverage before the first dose.

Medium-dose MART may remain the inhaled regimen with Step 5 add-ons. A previous ICU admission, triple inhaler, or biologic use alone does not confirm severe asthma.
